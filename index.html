The 65-Bag Rule: When Bulk Mulch Finally Beats Bags

Mulch is sold in bags, but contractors measure it in cubic yards — two different languages, and the translation decides whether bags or bulk is cheaper. The break-even point is about 65 bags, and here is the math.

One cubic yard equals exactly 27 cubic feet, so it takes 13.5 standard 2.0 cu ft bags to make one cubic yard — round up to 14 in the cart. At the standard 3-inch depth, one cubic yard covers 108 square feet.

Take a real bed: 30 ft long by 10 ft wide at 3 inches deep. That is 300 sq ft, which needs 2.78 cubic yards. Add the usual 10% waste allowance for uneven ground and settling and you get 3.06 cubic yards, or 82.5 cubic feet. In bags that is 42 bags of the standard 2.0 cu ft size at $3.98 each, or $167.16. Bulk is $38 per yard, but 3.06 yards plus the $75 delivery fee totals $191.28 — so for this bed, bags win.

The math flips at about 4.8 cubic yards. Bags cost $53.73 per cubic yard against $38 bulk, a saving of $15.73 per yard, and the $75 delivery fee eats that saving until the job passes roughly 65 bags. Below that line, grab bags at the store. Above it, order bulk from a local landscape supply yard and have it dumped on a tarp on the driveway. A pickup truck only holds about 1.5 to 1.75 cubic yards heaped, so hauling a big job yourself means many trips. Order bulk, spread it within a few days, and keep a few spare bags sealed in the garage for touch-ups.
